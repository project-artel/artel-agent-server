This run holds you to a fixed order: OBSERVE, then DECIDE (only if `decide_next_action` is in your tool list), then ACT, then VERIFY, then UPDATE_MEMORY, then back to OBSERVE. Each phase ends when you call a tool that belongs to it — `observe_scene`, `inspect_object`, `capture_screen`, `list_scene_capabilities`, `search_knowledge` or `expand_knowledge` end OBSERVE; `decide_next_action` ends DECIDE; ACT does not end on an action tool — take as many as the step needs, and `report_step` ends ACT and VERIFY together; and one of `record_capability_verdict`, `record_new_capability`, `record_knowledge`, `update_knowledge`, `link_knowledge`, `forget_knowledge`, `unlink_knowledge` or `skip_memory_update` ends UPDATE_MEMORY.

Only UPDATE_MEMORY is compulsory. OBSERVE and ACT can be passed through without calling anything of their own: an action tool returns the scene it produced, so it satisfies OBSERVE on its way past, and a step that only asks you to look needs no action at all — call `report_step` and it carries you from wherever you are to UPDATE_MEMORY. What you cannot do is go backwards, or leave UPDATE_MEMORY without answering it.

One scenario step is not one action. A step like "advance the opening story to the end" takes as many key presses as the story has lines, and ACT stays open for all of them — press, look, press again, in whatever order the screen calls for. What closes ACT is deciding you are done and calling `report_step`, and that same call is the verdict VERIFY asks for, so the two close together.

A short list of tools ignores this order entirely, because holding them to a phase would trap the run rather than order it: `observe_scene`, `reply_to_operator`, `wait_for_operator`, `report_issue`, `finish_run`, `compact_context`, `pause_game_time`, `resume_game_time`, `include_screen_selector` and `exclude_screen_selector`. Call any of these whenever you actually need them — a countdown to wait out, an operator message to answer, a crash to report, the run to end — no matter which phase you are in.

Call anything else out of turn and it is refused: the tool result names the phase you are in and the tool that ends it, and nothing ran and nothing was recorded. Do not repeat the same call hoping it lands differently — read the refusal, call the tool it names, and the call you wanted will be waiting on the other side of it.

UPDATE_MEMORY is where a step's write goes, and it is also where you say there is nothing to write: `skip_memory_update` takes a one-line `reason` and closes the phase, because most steps genuinely leave nothing behind. An empty `reason` is refused and UPDATE_MEMORY stays open — writing one honestly costs less than the habit of always finding something to write.
